### Additional checks (all pass, 51/51)

**Normalization contract**
- `normalize` is idempotent.
- Outer rings have positive Y-down shoelace area and holes negative.
- Every ring starts at its lexicographically smallest (x, y).
- Parts are sorted by (minY, minX), with a total-order tie-break.
- Hole order, duplicate vertices, collinear vertices and 180° spikes do not change the hash.
- Zero-area parts are dropped.

**Hash scope**
- Layer index changes the hash.
- The multi-layer stream does not depend on input layer order.
- Score paths do not depend on direction, pass-through points or order.
- Closed score rings do not depend on start vertex or direction.
- The registration-hole section is order-invariant but sensitive to radius.
- Empty layers are well defined.

**Guards**
- Non-integer coordinates throw.
- Coordinates beyond ±2^25 µm throw. There is never silent truncation.
- `SBHash.sha256` matches `node:crypto`.

**D3 (saddles)**
- A figure-8 ring hashes the same as two vertex-touching squares.
- A pinched outer hashes the same as an outer plus a vertex-touching hole.
- A diamond hole touching the outer at its edge midpoints (T-contacts) hashes the same as four corner triangles. This also holds when the shape comes from Clipper's `Difference`, whose output both self-touches and T-touches.
- A "hole" wound like its outer is treated as material (NonZero).

**Strong invariance through a real boolean engine**
- 40 saddle-heavy 12×10 masks (509 saddles): Clipper unions of the traced loops and of per-pixel squares produced byte-different raw outputs in 40 of 40 cases, and identical canonical hashes in **40 of 40**.
- Stress test: 200 random masks (24×20 and 40×30, density 0.35–0.65). Four Clipper routes (PolyTree or flat × trace loops or pixel squares), each with ring rotation, global winding flips and part/hole shuffles, gave identical hashes in **200 of 200** masks. No normalized ring revisits a vertex.
- EvenOdd and NonZero agree on traced input. Repeated runs are identical.

**NFR-05 static scan.** `canon.js` contains no `Math.sin/cos/tan/atan/exp/log/pow/cbrt/sqrt/hypot/random`. The only division is grid binning inside `splitTJunctions`, which never reaches the output.

**Cross-engine.** The fixture is the draft 8-layer project: 1,660 Clipper-output parts, 70 of them exercising the contact/faceTrace path, and 2,739 rings that needed dedupe or collinear cleanup. All 8 layer hashes and the project hash are byte-identical across three engines:
- Node 26 (V8);
- headless Chromium 152 (V8);
- headless Firefox 155 (SpiderMonkey).

Project hash: `ee31340d4f239a4db609a39177e74c35298f418b07629db60ba39fd81062694a`.

## 4. Findings that shaped the design

1. **Rotate, reverse and sort alone are not canonical.** Clipper returns the same point set in different ring structures, depending on how the input was decomposed:
   - one outer that touches itself at saddles;
   - an outer plus holes that touch it at 1 to 4 vertices;
   - holes that touch each other;
   - vertex-on-edge (T) contacts.

   A naive normalize matched the trace route and the pixel route in only 37 of 40 masks. The fix makes D3 a construction rule, not just a validation rule. Per polygon:
   1. `cleanRing` removes duplicate, collinear and spike vertices.
   2. Roles come from orientation relative to the outer (NonZero).
   3. `splitTJunctions` inserts T-contact vertices.
   4. `faceTrace` re-chains every directed edge. At a shared vertex it leaves by the left-most turn (hugging the material), which is a fixed permutation of the edges.
   5. `splitRing` splits a component that touches itself around an enclosed hole.
   6. Rings are classified by sign, and each hole is assigned to its smallest containing outer.

   A fast path skips steps 3–6 when no vertex is shared: 1,590 of 1,660 parts in the fixture.
2. **Ring roles must follow orientation, not tree position.** The (patched) Clipper2-js PolyTree reported a diagonally isolated single-pixel island as a *hole* of the enclosing ring. Trusting tree roles corrupts the result. NonZero semantics are what §3 mandates anyway: a ring's role is its sign relative to the outer.
3. **Clipper2 output always needs cleaning.** With `preserveCollinear` on by default, it emits consecutive duplicate points, collinear points and `-0` coordinates. Every one of 2,739 fixture rings changed under `cleanRing`. `-0` is harmless because `setInt32(-0)` writes 0, but G1 must not compare raw Clipper output with `JSON.stringify`/`Object.is`.
4. **There is a guide/hash cycle in the plan as written.** G3.3 says guides carry the `canonicalHash` of both layers and are scored onto layer k. The S6 interface puts `scorePaths` inside `canonicalBytes([layer])`. If `canonicalHash` includes score paths, a layer's hash depends on guides that depend on that hash. Resolution: two hashes (D4 below).

## 5. Decision D4 — hash scope (proposed)

1. **Canonical form** (`SBGeom.normalize`):
   - integer µm, Y-down;
   - outer rings positive and holes negative;
   - each ring starts at its smallest (x, y);
   - no duplicate, collinear or spike vertices;
   - vertex and T contacts resolved into simple rings, with point contact never connecting material (D3);
   - holes and parts sorted by (bbox minY, bbox minX) and then by the full coordinate sequence (a total order).

   Input must be boolean-resolved: `normalize` does not merge overlapping or edge-sharing parts. `canonicalBytes` always normalizes, which is idempotent and cheap on normalized input, so the hash never depends on the caller having normalized.
2. **Byte stream** (little-endian int32): `[layerCount, (index, partCount, (ringCount, (vertexCount, x, y…)…)…, scoreCount, (vertexCount, x, y…)…, holeCount, (cx, cy, r)…)…]`. Layers are sorted by index.
   - **Amendment A:** add the trailing `holeCount, (cx, cy, r)` section. Registration holes are already subtracted into `material` (G1.3), but G3.1 requires holes to enter the hash. A hole that misses material, or is moved by less than its own effect, should still change the hash.
   - Score paths are canonicalized as follows: dedupe, drop pass-through collinear points (reversals are kept), and orient so the smaller endpoint comes first. Closed paths are oriented as rings. Score paths are then sorted.
3. **Two layer hashes** (**Amendment B**, which breaks the guide cycle):
   - `materialHash(k) = sha256(canonicalBytes([{index, material, holes}]))` with `scoreCount = 0`. This is stored as `MaterialLayer.canonicalHash` and is used by guide invalidation (SUP-05, `part.guideRefs`), by `Repair.reviewed.beforeHash`/`afterHash`, by MAT-04 and by part-ID stability.
   - `layerHash(k) = sha256(canonicalBytes([{index, material, holes, scorePaths}]))`. Only this one feeds `geometryHash.layers`.
4. **`geometryHash`** stays as in §3: `hashJSON({key: geometryKey(p), engine: engine.version, quality, raster, layers: layerHashes, guides: guideHash})`.
   - `layerHashes` holds one entry per index 0..N−1, including empty and omitted-trailing layers. An empty layer hashes `[1, k, 0, 0, 0]`.
   - Because score polylines are already in `layerHash`, `guideHash` covers only the labels, omissions and reasons, and the map.
   - The full multi-layer `canonicalBytes(allLayers)` is **not** hashed for `geometryHash`, because hashing the layer hashes is equivalent and halves the cost. It stays available for packaging.
5. **Excluded from every geometry hash:**
   - derived data: `parts` and IDs, `cutPaths`, `stats`, `diagnostics` and `zBottomMM`/`zTopMM` (the last two come from `geometryKey`);
   - `appearance`, `view`, `acks`, `extras`, `title` and `units`, as §3 already says.

   `carriers` is always `[]` in v1.0. If it is ever populated, append a section and bump `engine.version`.
6. **Determinism guards** (**Amendment C**):
   - `canonicalBytes` throws on non-integer coordinates and on |v| > 2^25 µm (33.5 m). That bound keeps every cross product an exact double; the plan's int32 alone does not guarantee this.
   - No transcendental math is used, and the only division is for bucketing.
   - Any change to normalization or byte layout requires an `engine.version` bump. Persisted `materialHash` values then simply mismatch, so repairs fail closed (`REPAIR_STALE`).

## 6. Notes for S1 (geometry backend) from this spike

These notes concern clipper2-js 1.2.4 (TS port, last pushed 2024-01-01):
- **PolyTree is broken.** `OutRec` never initializes `bounds`/`path`, so `executePolyTree` always fails (it returns `false` after an internal `TypeError`). The spike uses a local monkey patch in `harness.mjs`. The vendor file is unchanged.
- **Shared mutable static.** `ClipperBase.getBounds` mutates the shared `Clipper.InvalidRect64` singleton.
- **Transcendental math in the library.** It contains `Math.sin/cos/atan2/log10/pow` in round-join offset, ellipse and Minkowski code. The adapter must keep refusing `"round"` (NFR-05).
- **The union is slow at stress scale** (33 s for 1.2M vertices), but it is well within budget at the SRS envelope.

The canonical layer makes the hash independent of whichever Clipper route or ring structure S1 picks. That holds for PolyTree or flat output, as long as the flat output is assembled by orientation (`assembleRings`).

## 7. Recommendation

1. Accept D4 as written in §5, including amendments A (hole section), B (`materialHash` versus `layerHash`) and C (coordinate bound).
2. Port `spikes/S6/canon.js` into `js/geom.js`: `normalize` = `cleanRing` → relative-orientation roles → `splitTJunctions` → `faceTrace` → `splitRing` → classify, then `canonicalBytes`.
3. Move the 51 checks into the S1/G1 suites under the `S6/…` and `D3 …` names, with the 200-mask four-route stress test as a property test.
4. Add the cross-engine fixture to the G4.8 browser harness.
5. In the engine, build each layer's canonical bytes once. Derive `materialHash` and `layerHash` from those bytes, sharing everything up to the score section, and compute `geometryHash` from the hash list.
