## Findings

### F1. "Split rings at repeated vertices" is not a normalization: it flips Clipper2's pairing (D3, G1.1, AT-06)

**Mechanism.** In the minimal case `##.#. / #.### / ##.## / ###..`, two 4-connected parts touch at three corner points: (2,1), (2,2) and (3,3), in pixel units. They form a cycle that encloses empty space. Clipper2's union returns **a single ring, with no holes, that passes through all three points** (20 vertices, 3 of them repeated). At each repeated vertex the ring has two incoming and two outgoing edges, and they can be paired in two ways:
- the **separating** pairing, where each incoming edge continues with the outgoing edge that bounds the same material wedge, so the two parts stay apart;
- the **joining** pairing, which crosses from one part's wedge to the other's.

Clipper2 chooses separating at (2,1) and (3,3) and joining at (2,2). That mix is why the result is one ring: with separating pairs at all three points it would be two rings.

Splitting a ring at a repeated vertex v (taking `r[j..i)` and the rest) **swaps the pairing at v**. The plan's rule splits at the first repeat found. That turns the separating pair at (2,1) into a joining one, and the resulting split puts the two visits to (2,2) into different sub-rings, so (2,2) is never revisited. The scan then splits at (3,3) and flips it too. The result is 1 outer ring plus 2 holes, joined at all three points: one polygon whose interior is in two pieces.

The outcome depends on Clipper2's arbitrary choice at each touch point and on the order of the scan. Across the sweeps, 1–14 % of Clipper2's continuations at shared vertices are joining in noise (none in the diamond-ring family), so the split-only rule fails on whole families of masks (table below).

**Correct rule (normative, implemented in `normalizeRings`):** apply it to the ring set of every boolean or offset result, globally across all rings.
1. **Node.** Insert into an edge any ring vertex that lies strictly inside it (a T-contact). Use exact integer collinearity, so that every contact is a shared vertex.
2. **Re-pair.** Discard Clipper2's ring order. At every vertex with two or more outgoing edges, continue each incoming edge with the **right-most** outgoing edge. With outer rings positive in Y-down, material lies to the right of travel, so the right-most turn is the outgoing edge that bounds the same material wedge. Compare turns exactly with a half-plane test and a cross product, with no trigonometry (NFR-05). A valid 0/1-winding boundary alternates incoming and outgoing edges around each vertex, so this pairing is a bijection.
3. **Split** any ring that still repeats a vertex. After step 2, both wedges at that vertex belong to the same part. Splitting therefore turns a self-touching outer ring into an outer ring plus a hole that touches it, and a self-touching hole into two holes. It never produces two outer rings.
4. Drop collinear vertices, fix orientation, rotate each ring to start at its minimum vertex, nest holes (exact point-in-ring test on doubled coordinates, with a grid index) and sort.

**Postcondition.** Every polygon is exactly one part. Rings inside a polygon may touch at isolated points without crossing (outer–hole and hole–hole contact). That is OGC-valid, because the interior stays connected.

**Exact one-part test (`interiorConnected`).** Take a polygon whose rings are simple and meet only at points. Build a bipartite graph whose nodes are rings and contact points (T-contacts included, found by noding), with an edge wherever a ring passes through a contact point. Then the interior has `1 + (cycle rank of that graph)` components; this follows from Alexander duality, because the complement is a union of disks glued at points. So the polygon is one part if and only if the graph is a forest. `components()` asserts this and throws `GEO_MULTIPART_POLYGON` otherwise. `validate()` reports `GEO_MULTIPART`.

**Evidence** (`s5_d3_property.mjs`, full run). The oracle requires a pixel-exact one-to-one match between output polygons and BFS 4-components. "Saddle cycles" is the cycle rank of the graph whose nodes are parts and whose edges are saddles. Multi-part cycles are the F1 trigger.

**Noding is defensive in the output path.** In every case tested, Clipper2 had already inserted the T-contact points inside one output group. **Smooth art rarely triggers F1.** `randomNestedStack` 120×90×6 had 7 saddles in 100 layers and no multi-part cycles. Dithered, noisy or hatched art triggers it constantly. Without the fix, SUP-01 support graphs and G1.1 part IDs would treat two parts as one.

### F3. Unpinned per-loop smoothing leaves sub-pixel holes at the frame edge (G1.2 and G1.3; limited evidence)

Test setup: `SBTrace.chaikin(loop, 2)` applied **to each loop independently**, at 0.25 mm/px with a 10 mm frame, on `borderTouch` and on 200 masks of 32×24 noise at p = 0.55. The "pinned" variant holds fixed any vertex that lies on the art rectangle. A "frame-edge micro-hole" is a hole ring that has a vertex on the art rectangle and an area below one pixel (62,500 µm²).

| Mode | `borderTouch` | 200 framed noise masks |
|---|---|---|
| raw | 1 hole | 0 frame-edge micro-holes |
| unpinned Chaikin | **one extra hole of 24,390 µm²** between the art and the frame | micro-holes on **all 200 masks** (521 in total), as small as **2,480 µm²** |
| Chaikin, art-boundary vertices pinned | 1 hole, same as raw | 538 frame-edge holes on 189 masks, all ≥ 57,299 µm². These are smoothed versions of 1-px raw notches. None is smaller. |

What this shows: unpinned smoothing pulls art corners off the frame line and creates slivers as small as 4 % of a pixel there, and pinning stops it. What it does **not** show:
- that G1.2's containment-aware smoother behaves the same way, since it was not available;
- that this pin rule is the right one;
- anything about topology away from the frame. On noise, per-loop Chaikin removed about 30 % of all holes compared with raw (−5,836 unpinned, −6,366 pinned), and the spike did not investigate that.

Part counts were unchanged in every mode. Treat F3 as a strong argument for a pin predicate and as a required G1.2/G1.3 test, not as proof.

### F4. Clipper2 `Paths64` offsets cannot express sub-µm insets (D3, G2.7, Appendix B.3) — corrected

Clipper2 rounds every offset vertex with `Math.round`, which sends ties toward +∞. `ClipperOffset.executeInternal` returns the input unchanged when `|delta| < 0.5`. Measured with miter joins on integer input (`s5_inset.mjs`, `results_inset.json` `clipperOffsetProbe`):

| delta | Result |
|---|---|
| −0.4, −0.49 | input returned **unchanged** (no-op) |
| **−0.5** | The result depends on orientation and shape. A positive 4000×10000 rectangle gives **`[]`**. The same rectangle with reversed ring order shrinks by 1 µm on two sides only. A positive L-shape gives a **wrong polygon of 2,497,000.5 µm²**, half of its true 5,000,000. A rectangle with a hole gives a malformed outer ring. A right triangle shrinks correctly by about 1 µm. A diamond comes back unchanged. |
| −0.51, −1 | shrinks by 1 µm on every side |
| −1.5 | shrinks by 2 µm on two sides and 1 µm on the other two |

Revision 1's "`[]` for any polygon" generalized from the one orientation it tried. The conclusion still holds, for a stronger reason: **a sub-µm `offset` call is meaningless on the 1 µm grid.** The spike adapter's `offset()` now rejects non-integer deltas (`GEO_OFFSET_NONINTEGER`). The S1 battery's offset checks use deltas of 500 µm or more and will not catch this, so add a check.

### F6. Finite-width contact: an exactly certified test (D3, G2.7, B.3)

**Definition.** For a support intersection I, r\*(I) is the radius of the largest closed disk contained in I, and the **contact width** is w = 2·r\*. "I survives an inward offset of d" means the closed erosion I ⊖ B(d) is non-empty, which holds exactly when r\* ≥ d. This makes Appendix B.3's two clauses consistent, because its warning already reads "does not survive `offset(−minFeatureUm/2)`, i.e. contact width below the full minimum feature width":
- **block** if w < 0.5 µm, i.e. I does not survive an inset of 0.25 µm. Point and line contact have an empty intersection and are covered by this.
- **warn** (`SUPPORT_NARROW`) if w < `minFeatureUm`, i.e. I does not survive an inset of `minFeatureUm/2`. A width exactly equal to the limit passes.

**Algorithm** (`insetStatus(I, d)` in `geom_s5.mjs`) has three outcomes.
- **SURVIVES — exact.** The test looks for a rational witness point p = (X/q, Y/q) that is strictly inside I by an even–odd test, with `dist(p, e)² ≥ d²` for every boundary edge e. Both checks run in BigInt, against a dyadic d. If both hold, then B(p, d) ⊆ I, so r\* ≥ d. That conclusion does not depend on Clipper2, floating point or the scale. Candidates come from Clipper2 insets at ×s on translated coordinates: miter and bevel at d·s ± 2 units, using centroids, vertices and edge midpoints. If none of those works, the four deepest candidates are refined with a float pattern search over 16 fixed directions and snapped to 1/4096 µm. Only miter, bevel, `+ − × ÷ √` and a fixed direction table are used, so the result is deterministic.
- **FAILS — certified through Clipper2.** The **bevel** inset at d − m is empty, where m = 2 units at ×s. A bevel inset contains the true inset, because its chords lie inside the disks. If r\* ≥ d, the inset at d − m would contain a disk of radius m around the witness. Clipper2 moves each vertex by at most 0.71 units when it rounds, so the result could not be empty. Therefore empty means r\* < d. Before giving up, the test tries a second, tighter outer bound, a round join with arc tolerance 0.25 units. That bound uses trigonometry, but it only separates FAILS from UNDECIDED. This certificate assumes that Clipper2 offsets by the positive-winding region of the raw offset path. The spike cross-checks that assumption but does not prove it from the code.
- **UNDECIDED** means neither of the above. **`survivesInset` returns true only for SURVIVES**, so UNDECIDED counts as "does not survive": the test warns or blocks, which is the safe direction. That makes the boolean **provably conservative**: it never reports a contact as wider than it is. Scale defaults to `max(16, ⌈16/d⌉)`, so ×64 at d = 0.25 and ×16 at d ≥ 1.

**Evidence** (`s5_inset.mjs`, full run). Every family uses an exact BigInt oracle except the last.

| Family | Cases | Wrong verdicts (default, ×4, ×16, ×64) | Undecided | Old method (miter at ×s, area > 0): wrong verdicts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| parallelograms, random slope, heights within ±max(1 µm, 0.2 %) of 2d, d ∈ {0.25, 1.5, 7, 1500} | 2,398 | **0** | 196, all with r\* < d and d − r\* ≤ 0.135 µm (≤ 2.2 grid units) | ×4: 16 wrong survive, 95 wrong fail. ×16: 5 / 27. ×64: 0 / 5. The scales disagree on 125. |
| axis rectangles, including ties w = 2d | 18 | **0** | 0 (ties are certified as surviving) | 10 wrong fail at every scale; a 3000 µm overlap at d = 1500 was flagged |
| diagonal slivers (I = triangle ∩ shifted triangle, normal width k/√2) | 9 | **0** | 0 | ×4: 2 wrong fail |
| L-shapes, r\* = (2 − √2)·w (the disk is limited by the reflex corner) | 84 | **0** | 0 | 44 of 48 that truly survive were reported as failing, at every scale |
| plus shapes, r\* = w/√2 (limited by four reflex corners) | 84 | **0** | 0 | 40 of 40 that truly survive were reported as failing, at every scale |
| intersections of random star polygons, d within ±3 % of a sampled lower bound r_lb ≤ r\* | 250 | 0 witnesses fail a float re-check; 0 "fails" with r_lb ≥ d | 0 | — |

Certified verdicts never contradicted each other across scales. Every UNDECIDED case so far had r\* < d, so the boolean equalled the truth on every exact-oracle case. Completeness is measured, not proven: a true contact can be reported as not surviving only within that undecided band.

**B.3 classification, `classifyContact(I, minFeatureUm = 3000)`:**

| Contact | Level |
|---|---|
| point or line contact (empty intersection) | block |
| needle triangle 20001 × 1 µm (inradius 2.5·10⁻⁵ µm) | block |
| 1 µm axis overlap | warn |
| diagonal sliver of normal width 0.707 µm | warn |
| 2999 µm overlap | warn |
| 3000 µm overlap | **ok** |

With integer-µm vertices, every axis-aligned non-empty overlap is at least 1 µm wide. Only empty intersections and sub-0.5 µm slivers from non-axis geometry (smoothing, clipping) can block.

**Cost.** On `randomNestedStack` 1536×1024×8, seeds 1–2, 8 overlapping part pairs: on average 2–6 ms per pair for the intersection and 11–17 ms for the classification. On a staircase contact of 8,000 vertices near the threshold: 120–260 ms. The bevel failure check runs before the refinement, which keeps the "fails" path cheap.

### F5. Performance

Pipeline measured: trace → `fromPixelLoops` → union + normalize (node, re-pair, split, nest) → frame union → `components()` (which now runs `interiorConnected`), plus the `SBMorph` comparison.

| Workload | Result |
|---|---|
| `randomNestedStack(lcg(s), 1536, 1024, 8)`, s = 1..5, 40 layers, mean 268 vertices per layer | **whole 8-layer stack: p50 258 ms, p95 305 ms** |
| Per layer, p95 | trace 36 ms; union + normalize 14 ms; frame union 11 ms; `components` 1.8 ms; `SBMorph` comparison 51 ms |
| Worst case: uniform noise 1536×1024 (195k saddles, 108k loops, about 430k vertices) | trace 1.9 s; Clipper2 union alone 5.9 s; **union + normalize 19.0 s** (revision 1 took 11.8 s); `components` 2.9 s; 103,128 parts = 103,128 raster 4-components |

The global noding, the re-pair step, the string-keyed vertex maps and the grid nesting add about 13 s over Clipper2 alone in the worst case. A production `SBGeom` should:
- key vertices numerically, for example as `x * 2^26 + y`;
- skip noding when the vertex set has no collinear contact, which can be detected cheaply;
- normalize once per boolean.

Images like this should be stopped earlier by G2 cleanup (specks and minimum feature size), not by geometry.

## Recommendation

1. **Record D3 with this wording.** Write it into `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` when S1 and S5 merge, and replace the plan's §3 bullet "Rings that touch at a single vertex … are split into separate simple rings":
   - **Connectivity.** Material parts are 4-connected. Contact at a single point never joins two parts. `SBTrace.trace` already keeps diagonal contact separate, so **G1.1 needs no `SBMorph` pre-split.**

   - **Finite-width contact.** Width = 2·r\*. **Block if w < 0.5 µm** (does not survive an inset of 0.25 µm). **Warn `SUPPORT_NARROW` if w < `minFeatureUm`** (does not survive an inset of `minFeatureUm/2`).
